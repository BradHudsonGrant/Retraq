# Quick Journal / Journalistic battlecard — parity note

**Status:** Ship-ready · **Task:** 5.12 · **Last updated:** 2026-04-16

> **TL;DR:** Quick Journal and Journalistic occupy a minimalist
> "fast-entry" niche. Retraq is incrementally better than both but
> not differentiated on that axis. **Do NOT lead with "fast journal"
> in marketing.** Route those comparisons back to the Daylio
> battlecard's recall-first flank.

## The competitor at a glance

| Field | Quick Journal | Journalistic |
|---|---|---|
| Install base | <1M combined (Play + iOS est.) | Minimal |
| Core pitch | "Log a thought in seconds" | "Minimalist journal, no friction" |
| Price model | Free with IAP | Free |
| Platforms | iOS + Android (varies) | iOS |
| Moat | None — commoditized niche |

## The parity finding

Retraq's entry path (tap Jot tab → type → save) is objectively fast
— arguably on par with or faster than Quick Journal when the hero
prompt helps overcome the blank-page pause. But **speed of entry is
not a differentiator in 2026.** Every credible journal app ships
"open → log → done" in under 15 seconds. Anchoring Retraq's
narrative on this axis commoditizes the pitch and puts us in direct
price-comparison with Quick Journal's free tier.

## Do NOT lead with

- "The fastest journal app."
- "Log your thoughts in seconds."
- "Minimalist journaling."
- Any claim that implies speed-of-entry is the primary value prop.

These claims test the same across every competitor in the minimalist
niche; users who respond to them have no reason to pick Retraq.

## Do lead with (re-routing)

When a creator / reviewer / user asks "how is this different from
Quick Journal," route the answer through the **recall-first** frame:

> "Quick Journal is great if what you want is to *log a moment
> right now*. Retraq is different — it's built for when you open
> a journal app *not* to write, but to read your past self.
> Weekly recap, on-this-day-last-year, full-text search across
> everything. That's what Quick Journal doesn't do."

This is a verbatim variant of the Daylio Scenario B script from
`./daylio.md`. Identical positioning — we win on recall, we don't
pretend to win on entry speed.

## Store-listing copy guardrails

- **Do NOT use** "quick" or "fast" as the headline adjective
  anywhere in the App Store subtitle, Play short description, or
  onboarding copy.
- **Do NOT** A/B test subtitle variants that lead with speed.
- If a listing reviewer asks for a faster frame, push back with
  the recall framing.

## When speed-of-entry DOES come up

Two contexts where acknowledging speed parity is fine:

1. **Press / reviewer Q&A.** "Yes, Retraq's entry is fast — that's
   table stakes in this category. What makes Retraq different is
   what you see *before* you write: the recap of last week, the
   surface of recent entries, the 'on this day' recall."
2. **Onboarding hint.** A single onboarding copy element can say
   "One sentence is enough" — that's a tone/pressure statement,
   not a speed claim. It works because the pair phrase is
   "memory you can keep," which lands as warm, not competitive.

## Why this battlecard is short

Because **the positioning work is to NOT engage this niche**.
Documenting that explicitly is the whole artifact. If this card
starts growing — new scenarios, new DM scripts, new objection
handling — it's probably drifting toward the thing it's here to
prevent. Route the energy into Daylio (recall depth) or Rosebud/
Mindsera (AI-as-enhancement) battlecards instead.
