## The concurrency bug (Task 6.4, commit `c5beccd`)

Writing the concurrency coverage for `LockoutController` surfaced a
real production bug that wasn't in the audit.

`recordFailedAttempt` scheduled `_startLockout()` whenever
`_failedAttempts >= maxAttempts`, unconditionally. Under N concurrent
calls crossing the threshold (rapid double/triple-tap of a wrong
PIN), each scheduling ran its sync prelude — including
`_lockoutCount++` — before any awaited the persistence write. 10
concurrent failures produced `_lockoutCount == 6`, which multiplied
the NEXT lockout's exponential-backoff window by up to 6×.

User-visible impact: a rapid double/triple-tap of a wrong PIN on
the 5th attempt could lock the user out for 15–30 minutes instead
of 30 seconds.

Fix: gate `_startLockout()` on `!_isLockedOut`. Exactly one call
crosses the threshold; subsequent concurrent callers observe the
active window and short-circuit without compounding.

This is the kind of thing integration tests at the system level
rarely catch — the fast-lane Dart concurrency test found it in
under 10 minutes.

## The coverage-filter bug finding

The 45% CI floor had been running on a silently-broken filter
for months — generated files' DA: lines were still counted in
both numerator and denominator, just without their SF: header.
Effect: measured coverage was wrong (too low) because codegen
output dominated the total-line count without contributing hits.

Fix is a one-awk-block diff. Local reading now 48% filtered (was
43% under the broken filter; real hand-written-code coverage was
always higher than the measurement reflected). Floor raised from
45% to 47% in 6e1a29d with 1% margin for minor regressions.
